### Task 1: Phonetic R-Sound Classification (Written)

**Classification Definitions (Modern RP / SSBE)**:
- **[NR] Non-Rhotic**: The letter 'r' is present in the spelling, but is **silent** because it occurs before a consonant sound or at the end of an utterance preceding silence or a consonant. The preceding vowel is lengthened or turns into a centring diphthong/schwa.
- **[LR] Linking /r/**: The letter 'r' is present in the spelling at the end of a word or morpheme, and is **pronounced** because the immediately following word or syllable begins with a vowel sound.
- **[IR] Intrusive /r/**: There is **no letter 'r' in the spelling**, but an `/r/` sound is inserted between words when the first word ends in `/ə/`, `/ɔː/`, or `/ɑː/` and the subsequent word begins with a vowel sound.

---

#### 1. Sentence 1
> *The media <u>[ **IR** ]</u> alert caused widespread panic.*

- **Classification**: **[IR] Intrusive /r/**
- **Phonetic Realization**: `/ðə ˈmiː.di.ə-r-əˈlɜːt/`
- **Explanation**: *media* ends in `/ə/` with no 'r' in spelling; *alert* starts with `/ə/`. An intrusive `/r/` bridges the hiatus.

---

#### 2. Sentence 2
> *I have no idea <u>[ **IR** ]</u> of where my brothe<u>r [ **LR** ]</u> is living.*

- **idea [ IR ] of**: `/aɪˈdɪə-r-əv/` ➔ **[IR] Intrusive /r/** (*idea* has no 'r' in spelling).
- **brother [ LR ] is**: `/ˈbrʌð.ər-ɪz/` ➔ **[LR] Linking /r/** (*brother* has an 'r' in spelling, activated before the vowel of *is*).

---

#### 3. Sentence 3
> *The govern<u>[ **NR** ]</u>ment tried to play down the disaste<u>r [ **LR** ]</u> and move fo<u>r [ **NR** ]</u>ward.*

- **govern[ NR ]ment**: `/ˈɡʌv.ən.mənt/` ➔ **[NR] Non-Rhotic** ('r' is silent before the consonant /n/ or /m/).
- **disaster [ LR ] and**: `/dɪˈzɑː.stər-ənd/` ➔ **[LR] Linking /r/** ('r' in spelling precedes the vowel in *and*).
- **for[ NR ]ward**: `/ˈfɔː.wəd/` ➔ **[NR] Non-Rhotic** ('r' is silent before consonant /w/).

---

#### 4. Sentence 4
> *She was standing fa<u>r [ **LR** ]</u> away from the ca<u>r [ **LR** ]</u> insurance office.*

- **far [ LR ] away**: `/fɑːr-əˈweɪ/` ➔ **[LR] Linking /r/** (terminal spelled 'r' glides into *away*).
- **car [ LR ] insurance**: `/kɑːr-ɪnˈʃʊə.rəns/` ➔ **[LR] Linking /r/** (spelled 'r' connects to the initial vowel of *insurance*).
  *(Note: In isolation, "car" is non-rhotic `/kɑː/`, but before the vowel of "insurance", the linking /r/ is fully activated).*

---

#### 5. Sentence 5
> *Let us draw <u>[ **IR** ]</u> a conclusion from the raw <u>[ **IR** ]</u> evidence.*

- **draw [ IR ] a**: `/drɔː-r-ə/` ➔ **[IR] Intrusive /r/** (*draw* ends in long vowel `/ɔː/` with no spelled 'r').
- **raw [ IR ] evidence**: `/rɔː-r-ˈev.ɪ.dəns/` ➔ **[IR] Intrusive /r/** (*raw* ends in `/ɔː/` with no spelled 'r').

### Task 3: Discourse Argumentation Writing

**Prompt**: *A 5-sentence opinion piece on modern news consumption using at least 4 target phrases.*

**Model Answer**:
```text
Title: The Algorithmic Architecture of Outrage

In contemporary political journalism, digital media platforms deliberately design their algorithms to stir up emotional outrage rather than foster nuanced civil discourse. Rather than offering balanced context, news networks consistently zero in on sensationalist culture war clashes that keep ideological opponents at loggerheads. Whenever independent watchdog organizations call out obvious algorithmic bias, corporate executives attempt to play down the societal fallout. This relentless sensationalism reinforces deeply entrenched views on both sides of the spectrum, eroding public trust in democratic institutions. Ultimately, until consumers demand substantive investigative reporting over viral clickbait, our collective media ecosystem will remain deeply dysfunctional.
```

*Expressions Integrated*:
1. **Stir up**
2. **Zero in on**
3. **At loggerheads**
4. **Call out**
5. **Play down**
6. **Entrenched views**

### Task 4: The 60-Second "Devil's Advocate" Challenge (Model Transcript)

> **Prompt Reference**: *"Argue that public complacency is actually a positive sign of a stable, prosperous society, and that when citizens get too engaged in politics, it only leads to chaos. Speak smoothly, using at least two target phrases and British stress-timed cadence."*

**Model Native Speaker Response (60-second delivery)**:
> *"While modern commentators frequently **call out** public complacency as a societal failing, **to be fair**, a high degree of political apathy is often the hallmark of a flourishing civilization. When citizens are constantly out in the streets and political factions remain **at loggerheads** over every minor policy adjustment, it signals deep institutional insecurity. By contrast, when the general public is relatively complacent, it indicates that basic infrastructure, the legal system, and commercial markets are functioning so reliably that ordinary people don't need to worry about them on a daily basis. When activists attempt to **stir up** continuous ideological conflict, it hardens **entrenched views** and paralyzes pragmatic governance. Therefore, far from being a moral catastrophe, a calm, complacent public is the greatest evidence that a state is fundamentally stable and secure."*
