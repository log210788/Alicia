### Task 1: Elision & Gemination Marking (Written)

**Rules & Notation**:
- **Parentheses** `(...)`: The alveolar stop `/t/` or `/d/` is **elided (dropped)** because it is flanked by consonants across a word boundary.
- **Ligature Arc** (`‿`): Identical consonants merge into a single prolonged **geminated** stop/fricative without a double release.

---

#### 1. Sentence 1
> *Las(t) week an ol(d) friend told me that we mus(t) not cut corners.*

- **Elisions**:
  - `Las(t) week`: `/t/` elided between `/s/` and `/w/` ➔ `/lɑːs wiːk/`
  - `ol(d) friend`: `/d/` elided between `/l/` and `/fr/` ➔ `/əʊl frend/`
  - `mus(t) not`: `/t/` elided between `/s/` and `/n/` ➔ `/mʌs nɒt/`
- **Connected Notes**:
  - *cut corners*: In rapid native RP speech, the final `/t/` of *cut* undergoes glottal replacement (`[kʌʔ ˈkɔːnəz]`) before the velar stop `/k/`.

---

#### 2. Sentence 2
> *The Prime ‿ Minister hel(d) back his speech until nex(t) Monday.*

- **Elisions**:
  - `hel(d) back`: `/d/` elided between `/l/` and `/b/` ➔ `/hel bæk/`
  - `nex(t) Monday`: `/t/` elided between `/ks/` and `/m/` ➔ `/neks ˈmʌndeɪ/`
- **Geminations**:
  - `Prime ‿ Minister`: The final `/m/` of *Prime* merges seamlessly into the initial `/m/` of *Minister* ➔ `/praɪˈmɪnɪstə/` (held nasal [mː], not released twice).

---

#### 3. Sentence 3
> *Don't dare drink tha(t) hot ‿ tea while you look ‿ closely at the bad ‿ dog.*

- **Elisions**:
  - `tha(t) hot`: The `/t/` is either elided or replaced with a light glottal stop before `/h/`.
- **Geminations**:
  - `hot ‿ tea`: Terminal `/t/` meets initial `/t/` ➔ single held closure: `[hɒtːiː]`
  - `look ‿ closely`: Terminal `/k/` meets initial `/k/` ➔ single held velar closure: `[lʊkːləʊsli]`
  - `bad ‿ dog`: Terminal `/d/` meets initial `/d/` ➔ single held voiced alveolar stop: `[bædːɒɡ]`

---

#### 4. Sentence 4
> *It was firs(t) class work, but the client was hel(d) back by budget limits.*

- **Elisions**:
  - `firs(t) class`: `/t/` elided between `/s/` and `/kl/` ➔ `/fɜːs klɑːs/`
  - `hel(d) back`: `/d/` elided between `/l/` and `/b/` ➔ `/hel bæk/`

---

#### 5. Sentence 5
> *Stop ‿ please, let us iron ‿ out the details before we hammer ‿ out a contract.*

- **Geminations**:
  - `Stop ‿ please`: Terminal bilabial stop `/p/` merges with initial `/p/` ➔ held closure: `[stɒpːliːz]`
- **Connected Linking Notes**:
  - `iron ‿ out`: Linking `/n/` into vowel ➔ `/ˈaɪən-naʊt/`
  - `hammer ‿ out`: Non-rhotic linking /r/ glide ➔ `/ˈhæmər-raʊt/`

### Task 3: Professional Business Scenario Writing

**Prompt**: *Draft a 4-to-6 sentence email to a demanding client who wants to launch immediately without QA testing, using at least 4 target expressions.*

**Model Answer**:
```text
Subject: Update on Project Milestone & Quality Assurance Testing

Dear Mr. Reynolds,

Thank you for your update regarding our pending release schedule. While I fully appreciate your commercial enthusiasm to launch ahead of time, releasing the platform without end-to-end QA testing would force us to cut corners on user data security. It was touch and go during our initial staging phase, and we should take early automated test results with a pinch of salt until human regression testing is complete. Having weighed up the operational risks against immediate delivery, our engineering team needs two extra days to iron out the remaining edge-case bugs and hammer out a final deployment protocol. We are confident this short delay will safeguard our reputation and ensure a flawless rollout.

Warm regards,

Alicia M.
Senior Project Lead
```

*Expressions Integrated*:
1. **Cut corners**
2. **Touch and go**
3. **Take with a pinch of salt**
4. **Weigh up**
5. **Iron out**
6. **Hammer out**

### Task 4: The 60-Second Spontaneous Dilemma Sprint (Model Transcript)

> **Prompt Reference**: *"When is it acceptable in a professional environment to cut corners, if ever? Or should quality and thoroughness always take absolute priority, even if deadlines are missed? Weigh up both perspectives and conclude with your opinion using at least two target expressions."*

**Model Native Speaker Response (60-second delivery)**:
> *"When looking at deadline pressures in business, deciding whether to **cut corners** is never straightforward. On one hand, in fast-moving tech environments where speed to market is paramount, perfectionism can paralyze an organization, so leaders must occasionally **weigh up** delivering an imperfect beta product against missing a commercial window entirely. However, when it comes to system architecture, cybersecurity, or contractual compliance, cutting corners is an unacceptable gamble. It might look like a clever shortcut initially, but you usually have to take short-term gains **with a pinch of salt**. In my experience, if you rush delivery, the situation inevitably becomes **touch and go**, and you end up spending twice as much time trying to **iron out** severe defects post-launch. Quality must always remain the non-negotiable bedrock of strategic leadership."*
