### Task 1: Weak Form Phonetic Identification (Written)

**Linguistic Rule**:
- **Weak Form**: Prepositions, auxiliary verbs, and conjunctions in unstressed positions reduce vowels to `/ə/`, `/ɪ/`, or `/ʊ/`.
- **Strong Form**: Occurs when the word is:
  1. At the **end of a clause or sentence** (stranded prepositions).
  2. Coordinated with another preposition (e.g. *by and for*).
  3. Emphatic, contrastive, or stranded auxiliary.

---

#### 1. Sentence 1
> *Where were you coming **from** [ **Strong: /frɒm/** ]? I was coming **from** [ **Weak: /frəm/** ] London.*

- **Explanation**:
  - The first *from* is at the end of the question (stranded preposition), so it takes the citation **Strong Form** `/frɒm/`.
  - The second *from* sits in unstressed rhythm before the noun *London*, so it reduces to the **Weak Form** `/frəm/`.

---

#### 2. Sentence 2
> *What are you looking **at** [ **Strong: /æt/** ]? I am looking **at** [ **Weak: /ət/** ] the data.*

- **Explanation**:
  - The first *at* is stranded clause-finally: **Strong Form** `/æt/`.
  - The second *at* precedes the noun phrase *the data*: **Weak Form** `/ət/`.

---

#### 3. Sentence 3
> *This report was written **by** and **for** [ **Strong: /fɔː/** ] senior executives.*

- **Explanation**:
  - *for* is paired and coordinated with *by* (*by and for*), receiving contrastive rhetorical focus: **Strong Form** `/fɔː/`.

---

#### 4. Sentence 4
> *He **can** [ **Weak: /kən/** ] speak three languages, but nobody thought he **could** [ **Strong: /kʊd/** ].*

- **Explanation**:
  - The first *can* is an unstressed auxiliary directly preceding the main verb *speak*: **Weak Form** `/kən/`.
  - The final *could* is stranded at the end of the clause with an omitted elliptical verb phrase: **Strong Form** `/kʊd/`.

---

#### 5. Sentence 5
> *A cup **of** [ **Weak: /əv/** ] tea **for** [ **Weak: /fə/** ] you and **for** [ **Weak: /fə/** ] me.*

- **Explanation**:
  - *of* is unstressed: **Weak Form** `/əv/`.
  - *for* before pronouns (*for you*, *for me*) standardly reduces to **Weak Form** `/fə/` in British English rhythm, unless special contrastive emphasis is placed on *you* versus *me*.

### Task 3: Blunt-to-Diplomatic British Rewriting (Written)

1. *"This graphic design is horrible and nobody in marketing will like it."*
   - **Target**: **Not quite what I had in mind** + **room for improvement**
   - **Model Answer**: *"To be fair, this visual direction is not quite what I had in mind, and I suspect there is still some room for improvement before we present it to marketing."*

2. *"Your consultant wants €400 an hour? That is crazy and we won't pay it."*
   - **Target**: **A bit steep** + **weigh up**
   - **Model Answer**: *"That day rate does seem a bit steep, so we will need to weigh up whether our current quarterly budget can accommodate it."*

3. *"I hate doing repetitive administrative tasks all day."*
   - **Target**: **Not really my cup of tea**
   - **Model Answer**: *"Spending the entire working day on repetitive administrative tasks is not really my cup of tea."*

4. *"We tried to launch in Germany but everything went wrong and failed."*
   - **Target**: **Didn't pan out** + **fell through**
   - **Model Answer**: *"Unfortunately, our initial expansion into the German market didn't pan out as expected, and several crucial commercial negotiations fell through."*

### Task 4: The 60-Second Diplomatic Pushback Sprint (Model Transcript)

> **Prompt Reference**: *"Your new company wants to assign you to lead a high-stakes client meeting completely alone during your very first week on the job. Push back diplomatically to your boss using at least two target understatement expressions and fluid weak forms."*

**Model Native Speaker Response (60-second delivery)**:
> *"Thank you for the opportunity to lead the client pitch next Tuesday; I'm genuinely delighted by the vote of confidence. However, **to be completely frank**, heading into such a high-stakes negotiation completely on my own during my very first week is **not quite what I had in mind** from a risk management perspective. While I've reviewed the background dossiers, the client's past commercial expectations are **a bit steep**, and if an unexpected legacy question arose, going in solo might **come across as** unprepared. I believe it would be far wiser to **weigh up** the reputational stakes and have an experienced account director join me. That way, we guarantee the deal doesn't **fall through** and we present a united, authoritative front."*
