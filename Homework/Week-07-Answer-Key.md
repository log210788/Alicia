### Task 1: Intonation Contour Mapping (Written)

**Intonation Contour Codes**:
- **`[ \ ]` Definitive Fall**: High-to-low pitch glide indicating finality, certainty, factual assertion, or authoritative closure.
- **`[ / ]` Open Rise**: Low-to-high pitch glide indicating non-finality, genuine questions (Yes/No), or openness.
- **`[ \/ ]` British Fall-Rise**: Fall followed immediately by a rise on the nuclear tone; indicates polite reservation, conditional nuance, hedged personal opinion, or concessive prefaces (*the quintessential British "diplomatic tune"*).

---

#### 1. Sentence 1
> *If you ask my opinion **[ \/ ]**, we shouldn't back down just yet **[ \ ]**.*

- `If you ask my opinion` ➔ **British Fall-Rise `[ \/ ]`** (introductory conditional hedge / polite reservation)
- `we shouldn't back down just yet` ➔ **Definitive Fall `[ \ ]`** (final declarative conclusion)

---

#### 2. Sentence 2
> *Would you be open to talking it through over lunch **[ / ]**?*

- **Open Rise `[ / ]`** (polite, non-imposing Yes/No inquiry)
- *(Note: A gentle Fall-Rise `[ \/ ]` on "lunch" is also natural in British RP to convey ultra-polite deference).*

#### 3. Sentence 3
> *Although he tried to smooth over the disagreement **[ \/ ]**, the client was still furious **[ \ ]**.*

- `Although he tried to smooth over the disagreement` ➔ **British Fall-Rise `[ \/ ]`** (dependent concessive clause / suspense)
- `the client was still furious` ➔ **Definitive Fall `[ \ ]`** (unambiguous factual assertion)

---

#### 4. Sentence 4
> *To be completely honest **[ \/ ]**, that strategy is skating on thin ice **[ \ ]**.*

- `To be completely honest` ➔ **British Fall-Rise `[ \/ ]`** (discourse marker introducing diplomatic warning)
- `that strategy is skating on thin ice` ➔ **Definitive Fall `[ \ ]`** (firm, serious judgment)

---

#### 5. Sentence 5
> *Let sleeping dogs lie **[ \/ ]**, otherwise we will never bury the hatchet **[ \ ]**.*

- `Let sleeping dogs lie` ➔ **British Fall-Rise `[ \/ ]`** (tactful advisory counsel / precondition)
- `otherwise we will never bury the hatchet` ➔ **Definitive Fall `[ \ ]`** (consequential warning)

### Task 3: Diplomatic Mediation Writing (Written)

**Prompt**: *A 5-to-7 sentence email to two feuding department heads inviting them to a neutral mediation session using at least 4 target phrases.*

**Model Answer**:
```text
Subject: Aligning on Strategy - Informal Sync

Dear Sarah and Mark,

Thank you both for your immense dedication to delivering the Q3 product release under such tight constraints. It has come to my attention that tensions have arisen regarding operational jurisdiction, and while our instinct might be to let sleeping dogs lie, I believe continuing without addressing this puts our broader roadmap on thin ice. Rather than allowing friction to fester, I would like to invite you both to an informal sync tomorrow afternoon so we can clear the air in a neutral environment. This will not be an exercise in finger-pointing, nor are we asking anyone to unreservedly back down from valid technical positions. Instead, this session will allow us to talk through our overlapping priorities, smooth over recent misunderstandings, and bury the hatchet so that we can lead our teams forward with complete synergy.

Warm regards,

Alicia M.
Chief Operating Officer
```

*Expressions Integrated*:
1. **Let sleeping dogs lie**
2. **Skate on thin ice**
3. **Clear the air**
4. **Back down**
5. **Talk through**
6. **Smooth over**
7. **Bury the hatchet**

### Task 4: The 60-Second De-escalation Sprint (Model Transcript)

> **Prompt Reference**: *"An aggressive, stressed colleague storms into your office and yells: 'I'm taking this directly to the Board to get that incompetent team fired!' De-escalate the situation immediately using calm British Fall-Rise intonation, diplomatic tact, and at least two target idioms."*

**Model Native Speaker Response (60-second delivery)**:
> *"Julian, please take a seat and take a breath for a second. **I completely understand your frustration \/**—the delays on the data migration have been immensely stressful for all of us. But going straight to the Board right now is **skating on thin ice \/**; it will only escalate hostility and make it impossible for us to resolve the root technical defect. If we bypass standard reporting channels, nobody wins, and leadership will be forced into a defensive posture. Instead of blowing this up into an institutional crisis, let's **clear the air** between ourselves right now. Give me twenty minutes to review the incident log with their lead architect. Once we **talk through** what actually broke down, we can decide whether formal escalation is truly necessary or if we can **smooth over** the workflow without unnecessary boardroom casualties. Let's handle this professionally."*
