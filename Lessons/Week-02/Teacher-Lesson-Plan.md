# Week 2: Teacher Lesson Plan
## Consonant Elision, Gemination & Articulatory Efficiency

- **Student**: Alicia M. (C1 Advanced)
- **Tutor**: Lewis O.
- **Duration**: 50 Minutes
- **Target Accent Model**: Modern Received Pronunciation (RP) / Standard Southern British English (SSBE)
- **Core Text Reference**: *Get Rid of your Accent (Part Two)*, Lesson 7: Consonant Elision (pp. 34–36).

---

## Lesson Objectives
By the end of this 50-minute session, Alicia will:
1. **Master Consonant Elision**: Naturally drop /t/ and /d/ when flanked by consonants across word boundaries (e.g., *nex(t) week*, *las(t) night*, *ol(d) friend*) to achieve effortless British flow.
2. **Execute Consonant Gemination**: Merge identical adjacent consonants with a single held articulation rather than releasing twice (e.g., *bus‿stop*, *bad‿dog*, *hot‿tea*).
3. **Eradicate Spanish Epenthetic /e/**: Produce clean initial /s/-clusters (*strategy*, *specific*, *structure*) without pre-voicing an intrusive Spanish /e/.
4. **Deploy British Corporate & Opinion Idioms**: Use *touch and go*, *cut corners*, *take with a pinch of salt*, *iron out*, *hammer out*, and *weigh up* in professional workplace contexts.
5. **Debate Professional Ethics & Workplace Shortcuts**: Spontaneously defend high standards vs. deadline realities.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Instructions & Exercise Banks

### Phase 1: Anti-Translation Activation & Speed Warm-Up (00:00 – 08:00)
**Tutor Goal**: Eradicate Spanish epenthetic /e/ (*eschool*) and establish rapid lexical retrieval under pressure.

#### Exercise 2.1: The Clean /s/ Rapid Sprint (3 mins)
*Spanish phonotactics forbid words beginning with /s/ + consonant, leading Spanish speakers to automatically insert /e/ ("eschool", "estart", "especial").*
*Drill: Tutor says the word; Alicia must hiss the /s/ for 1 second like a snake before releasing into the consonant with zero preceding vowel.*

1. `ssss-t-rategy` ➔ *strategy*
2. `ssss-p-ecific` ➔ *specific*
3. `ssss-t-art` ➔ *start*
4. `ssss-k-illful` ➔ *skillful*
5. `ssss-p-ontaneous` ➔ *spontaneous*
6. `ssss-t-akeholder` ➔ *stakeholder*

*Speed Integration*: Alicia must use *strategy* and *spontaneous* in a single 15-second sentence without stumbling or inserting an /e/.

#### Exercise 2.2: The "Under the Gun" 30-Second Dilemma (5 mins)
*Lewis presents an instant dilemma; Alicia has 2 seconds to begin speaking for 30 seconds straight without pausing to translate from Spanish.*
1. **Dilemma 1**: *"Your manager gives you a deadline that you know is physically impossible to meet without making errors. What do you do in the first 5 minutes?"*
2. **Dilemma 2**: *"A colleague takes credit for your strategic research during a high-stakes presentation. Do you call them out immediately or handle it privately?"*

### Phase 2: Pronunciation & Connected Speech Clinic (08:00 – 22:00)
**Target**: Consonant Elision of /t/ and /d/, and Consonant Gemination (Merging).

#### Theoretical Concept (2 mins)
Explain to Alicia:
- British speech avoids choppy articulatory stops.
- When /t/ or /d/ sits between other consonants, native speakers **drop (elide)** the sound entirely. *Last night* is pronounced */lɑːs-naɪt/*, NOT */lɑːst naɪt/*.
- When the same consonant ends one word and starts the next, we **hold** the sound once rather than popping the mouth twice. *Bus stop* is */bʌsːtɒp/*, NOT */bʌs - stɒp/*.

#### Exercise 2.3: Alveolar Stop Elision Drills (/t/ and /d/) (5 mins)
*(Adapted from James & Smith, Lesson 7, p. 34)*
*Read aloud. Cross out the silent /t/ or /d/ with your ear:*

**A. Word Pairs with /t/ Elision:**
- nex(t) ‿ door ➔ `/neks-dɔː/`
- las(t) ‿ night ➔ `/lɑːs-naɪt/`
- firs(t) ‿ class ➔ `/fɜːs-klɑːs/`
- don'(t) ‿ dare ➔ `/dəʊn-deə/`
- mus(t) ‿ be ➔ `/mʌs-biː/`
- fas(t) ‿ food ➔ `/fɑːs-fuːd/`

**B. Word Pairs with /d/ Elision:**
- ol(d) ‿ man ➔ `/əʊl-mæn/`
- col(d) ➔ col(d) ‿ weather ➔ `/kəʊl-weðə/`
- hel(d) ‿ back ➔ `/hel-bæk/`
- han(d) ‿ made ➔ `/hæn-meɪd/`
- frien(d) ‿ of ➔ *wait! Before a vowel /d/ is kept (catenation):* /fren-dəv/. *Before a consonant, it drops:* frien(d) ‿ ship.

**C. Elision Sentences:**
1. *She was the firs(t) ‿ person I saw las(t) ‿ night.*
2. *We mus(t) ‿ be careful not to push him nex(t) ‿ time.*
3. *An ol(d) ‿ friend told me that he hel(d) ‿ back the news.*
4. *Don'(t) ‿ dare cut corners on this projec(t) ‿ manager.*

#### Exercise 2.4: Consonant Gemination (Merging Identical Stops) (5 mins)
*Rule: When word A ends with the same consonant sound that word B begins with, do not separate them. Form the mouth shape and hold the pressure for an extra millisecond, gliding smoothly.*

**Word Pairs (James & Smith, p. 34):**
- hot ‿ tea ➔ `/hɒ-tiː/` (single held /t/)
- bad ‿ dog ➔ `/bæ-dɒɡ/` (single held /d/)
- bus ‿ stop ➔ `/bʌ-stɒp/` (single continuous /s/)
- Prime ‿ Minister ➔ `/praɪ-mɪnɪstə/` (single held /m/)
- look ‿ closely ➔ `/lʊ-kləʊsli/` (single held /k/)
- stop ‿ please ➔ `/stɒ-pliːz/` (single held /p/)
- with ‿ them ➔ `/wɪ-ðem/` (single held /ð/)

**Sentence Integration:**
1. *The Prime ‿ Minister made a quick ‿ call to stop ‿ please.*
2. *I sat at the bus ‿ stop drinking hot ‿ tea.*
3. *Look ‿ closely at that bad ‿ dog.*

### Phase 3: British Lexical Upgrade (22:00 – 32:00)
**Theme**: Workplace Agility, Deliberation & Pragmatism.

#### Core Lexicon Matrix

| Expression | Meaning & Nuance | Spanish Translation Pitfall | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Touch and go** | Precarious, uncertain, or risky outcome. | Literal: *"Tocar e ir"* (nonsense). Spanish speakers say *"it was risky"*. | *"The project approval was touch and go until the final vote."* |
| **Cut corners** | To do something sloppily or cheaply to save time/money. | Spanish speakers say *"take shortcuts"* or *"do badly"*. | *"We cannot afford to cut corners when security is at stake."* |
| **Take with a pinch of salt** | View information with healthy skepticism. | Spanish: *"Cogerlo con pinzas"* (similar concept, but English uses *pinch of salt*). | *"Take his promises with a pinch of salt; he tends to exaggerate."* |
| **Iron out** | Resolve minor technicalities or remaining issues. | Spanish speakers say *"solve the small problems"*. | *"We just need to iron out a few scheduling details."* |
| **Hammer out** | Reach an agreement through strenuous negotiation. | Spanish speakers say *"negotiate hard"* or *"make an agreement"*. | *"The two teams managed to hammer out a compromise."* |
| **Weigh up** | Carefully compare the advantages and disadvantages. | Spanish speakers say *"evaluate"* or *"ponderate"*. | *"Before accepting the job offer, she weighed up the pros and cons."* |

#### Exercise 2.5: Rapid Contextual Application (5 mins)
*Have Alicia transform each prompt using the target phrase:*
1. *Prompt*: "We need to solve these tiny contract details before tomorrow."
   ➔ *Alicia*: *"We need to **iron out** these last contract details before tomorrow."*
2. *Prompt*: "The outcome of the meeting was very uncertain and close to failing."
   ➔ *Alicia*: *"The meeting was truly **touch and go**."*
3. *Prompt*: "Don't believe everything marketing says in their pitch."
   ➔ *Alicia*: *"You should **take what marketing says with a pinch of salt**."*

### Phase 4: High-Level Roleplay Lab (32:00 – 47:00)
**Theme**: *The Product Launch: Quality Standards vs. Deadline Pressure.*

#### Scenario & Role Cards
- **Context**: Alicia is about to step into her new professional role. In this scenario, she is the **Senior Lead** managing an international client deliverable due in 48 hours. A critical software flaw has been discovered.
- **Alicia's Role**: Senior Project Lead. She refuses to cut corners because client trust is on the line. She must persuade the Operations Director to delay the launch by 3 days while the team irons out the bugs.
- **Lewis's Role**: Operations Director. He is obsessed with deadlines and penalties. He pushes Alicia to "patch it up quickly", ship it, and fix it later.

#### Script & Dialogue Triggers
- Alicia must incorporate:
  - Consonant elision: *nex(t) week, las(t) sprint, firs(t) priority*.
  - Gemination: *bad‿decision, quick‿call, hot‿water*.
  - Target vocabulary: *cut corners, iron out, touch and go, weigh up*.
- Lewis challenges her:
  - *"If we delay, our reputation takes an immediate hit. Can't we just cut corners on the testing phase?"*
  - *"Alicia, take their complaints with a pinch of salt—clients always panic before a release."*
- Alicia rebuts with calm British diplomatic firmness:
  - *"To be fair, if we release a broken product, it will be touch and go whether the client stays with us at all. We must weigh up the risks and hammer out a proper solution."*
