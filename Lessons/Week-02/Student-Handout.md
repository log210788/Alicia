## 1. Core Pronunciation Mechanics

### A. The Clean /s/ Onset (No Spanish /e/!)
In Spanish, words never begin with `s + consonant`, so Spanish speakers unconsciously add an "e" sound:
- ❌ *e-school, e-speak, e-strategy, e-specific*
- ✅ **ssss-chool, ssss-peak, ssss-trategy, ssss-pecific**

> **Exercise**: Hold the hissing `ssss` sound for one second before releasing into the word: `ssss... trategy`. Never let your vocal cords vibrate with an "e" beforehand!

### B. Consonant Elision (Dropping /t/ and /d/)
In rapid, connected British speech, when `/t/` or `/d/` occurs between two other consonants, it is dropped entirely to maintain smooth articulatory momentum:

- `las(t) ‿ night` ➔ /lɑːs-naɪt/
- `nex(t) ‿ door` ➔ /neks-dɔː/
- `firs(t) ‿ class` ➔ /fɜːs-klɑːs/
- `mus(t) ‿ be` ➔ /mʌs-biː/
- `ol(d) ‿ man` ➔ /əʊl-mæn/
- `hel(d) ‿ back` ➔ /hel-bæk/

### C. Consonant Gemination (Merging Identical Consonants)
When a word ends with the same consonant sound that begins the next word, do NOT release the sound twice. Hold the position for an extra split second and glide into the next vowel:

- `hot ‿ tea` ➔ /hɒ-tiː/ *(one sustained /t/)*
- `bus ‿ stop` ➔ /bʌ-stɒp/ *(one continuous /s/)*
- `bad ‿ dog` ➔ /bæ-dɒɡ/ *(one sustained /d/)*
- `Prime ‿ Minister` ➔ /praɪ-mɪnɪstə/ *(one sustained /m/)*
- `stop ‿ please` ➔ /stɒ-pliːz/ *(one sustained /p/)*

## 2. British Lexical Upgrade: Corporate Agility & Decision Making

| Expression | Definition | Natural British Workplace Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Touch and go** | Highly uncertain or precarious; on the brink of failure. | *"The merger was touch and go until the regulators approved it."* |
| **Cut corners** | To take cheap, sloppy shortcuts that risk quality or safety. | *"If we cut corners now, it will cost us double to fix later."* |
| **Take with a pinch of salt** | To view claims with healthy skepticism. | *"Take his revenue forecasts with a pinch of salt."* |
| **Iron out** | To solve minor details, technicalities, or friction points. | *"Let's set up a quick 10-minute call to iron out the schedule."* |
| **Hammer out** | To negotiate an agreement through intensive, determined discussion. | *"After five hours in the boardroom, we hammered out a compromise."* |
| **Weigh up** | To systematically compare advantages, disadvantages, and risks. | *"Before making any strategic moves, we need to weigh up all options."* |
