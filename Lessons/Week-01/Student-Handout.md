## 1. Core Pronunciation Concepts

### A. Stress-Timed Rhythm (British English vs. Spanish)
- **Spanish** is *syllable-timed*: each syllable receives equal length and energy.
- **British English** is *stress-timed*: only key content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) are stressed. Unstressed syllables are squashed and reduced to **Schwa (/ə/)**.

> **Pro Tip**: Keep your rhythm like music. Don't pronounce every letter clearly! Unstressed syllables should be "thrown away" quickly and softly.

### B. The Neutral Vowel: Schwa (/ə/)
- **Mouth position**: Jaw slightly open, tongue completely relaxed in the center, lips neutral. It is short and relaxed.
- **Practice Words**:
  - `c-o-mmunity` ➔ /kəˈmjuːnəti/
  - `s-u-ppose` ➔ /səˈpəʊz/
  - `o-bserve` ➔ /əbˈzɜːv/
  - `wom-a-n` ➔ /ˈwʊmən/
  - `c-o-nstruction` ➔ /kənˈstrʌkʃən/
  - `c-o-nvince` ➔ /kənˈvɪns/

- **British Endings with Schwa**:
  - Counties ending in *-shire* (/ʃə/): *Hampshire, Yorkshire, Derbyshire*
  - Towns ending in *-mouth* (/məθ/): *Bournemouth, Portsmouth, Dartmouth*

### C. Consonant-to-Vowel Catenation (`‿`)
When a word ends with a consonant sound and the following word begins with a vowel sound, link them together into a single continuous stream:

- `hold ‿ on` ➔ /həʊl-dɒn/
- `first ‿ of ‿ all` ➔ /fɜː-stə-vɔːl/
- `an ‿ apple` ➔ /ə-næp-əl/
- `turn ‿ out` ➔ /tɜː-naʊt/
- `brush ‿ off` ➔ /brʌ-ʃɒf/
- `pick ‿ it ‿ up` ➔ /pɪ-kɪ-tʌp/

## 2. British Lexical Upgrade: Idioms & Phrasal Verbs

| Expression | Meaning | Natural British Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **To be fair** | Introducing balance or conceding a fair point. | *"He made a mistake, but to be fair, he was under immense pressure."* |
| **At the end of the day** | The ultimate, unavoidable truth or bottom line. | *"At the end of the day, you have to do what's best for your career."* |
| **Quite frankly** | Expressing honest, direct sentiment politely but firmly. | *"Quite frankly, I think the proposed deadline is unrealistic."* |
| **A bit of a stretch** | Unlikely, exaggerated, or hard to accept. | *"Calling that minor glitch a 'catastrophe' is a bit of a stretch."* |
| **Come across (as)** | How one is perceived by other people. | *"In the interview, she came across as knowledgeable and articulate."* |
| **Turn out** | To result in a particular outcome. | *"We were worried about the weather, but it turned out to be glorious."* |
| **Brush off** | To dismiss or ignore something without taking offense. | *"She brushed off the negative comments and focused on her work."* |
