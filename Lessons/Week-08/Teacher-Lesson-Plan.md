### Phase 2: Pronunciation & Connected Speech Clinic (08:00 – 20:00)
**Target**: The 4 Ps Vocal Modulation System (James & Smith, Lessons 19–24, pp. 67–82).

#### Theoretical Concept (2 mins)
Explain the 4 Ps framework:
- A flat, monotonous voice loses an audience within 30 seconds.
- In British speech training (especially for actors, barristers, and corporate leaders), modulation is achieved through four distinct levers:
  1. **Power**: Not shouting, but projecting from the diaphragm; using quiet intensity for secrets/confidences and resonant projection for key announcements.
  2. **Pause**: The most powerful weapon. Pausing *before* a key word creates suspense; pausing *after* lets the impact settle. Eradicates "umm/err".
  3. **Pace**: Accelerating through background context and slowing down to deliberate, measured beats for core arguments.
  4. **Pitch**: Moving up and down musical registers to bring light and shade to speech.

#### Exercise 8.2: The 4 Ps Modulation Passage Reading (6 mins)
*(Adapted from James & Smith, pp. 69–81)*
*Have Alicia read this speech excerpt applying the annotated 4 P markers:*

> *(Pace: Quick, energetic)* "By and large, the challenges of the last year have tested our resilience across the board.  
> *(Pause: 2 seconds of total silence)* ...  
> *(Power: Quiet, focused resonance)* But our ability to adapt, to innovate, and to persevere ...  
> *(Pitch: Rising)* ... **bears testimony** to the extraordinary commitment of our team.  
> *(Pace: Slow, deliberate | Power: Commanding)* We cannot simply talk about change; we must **carry it out** ... and **follow through**."

#### Exercise 8.3: The "Zero-Monotony" Pitch Curve (4 mins)
*Take a mundane sentence and modulate it with extreme vocal dynamics:*
- Sentence: *"We have reviewed the quarterly numbers and decided to expand."*
  - Version 1: Whisper-quiet, intense focus (*Power: low, Pitch: low*).
  - Version 2: Dramatic pause before "expand" (*Pause: 2 seconds*).
  - Version 3: Rapid acceleration through "reviewed the quarterly numbers" (*Pace: fast*) followed by slow, punchy cadence on "decided to expand" (*Pace: slow*).

### Phase 3: British Lexical Upgrade (20:00 – 28:00)
**Theme**: Executive Leadership, Vision & Legacy Collocations.

#### Core Lexicon Matrix

| Expression | Meaning & Nuance | Spanish Translation Pitfall | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bear testimony to** | Serve as undeniable evidence of quality, effort, or truth. | Spanish speakers say *"give evidence of"* or *"demonstrate"*. | *"Our client retention figures bear testimony to our service quality."* |
| **Tip the balance** | Be the critical factor that decides a contested outcome. | Spanish: *"Inclinar la balanza"* (direct equivalent, highly polished in English). | *"Her exceptional pitch tipped the balance in our favor."* |
| **Set a precedent** | Establish a benchmark or standard that governs future actions. | Spanish: *"Sentar un precedente"*. | *"Granting a discount now will set a dangerous precedent."* |
| **By and large** | Speaking generally; taking everything into consideration. | Spanish speakers say *"in general"* or *"mostly"*. | *"By and large, the organizational transition has been remarkably smooth."* |
| **At the forefront** | In the leading, most innovative, or prominent position. | Spanish: *"A la vanguardia"*. | *"We want our firm to be at the forefront of sustainable finance."* |
| **Carry out** | Execute, conduct, or implement a task, plan, or audit. | Spanish speakers say *"realize"* (false friend from *realizar*). | *"We must carry out a comprehensive review before signing."* |
| **Follow through** | Complete a commitment or initiative to its final conclusion. | Spanish speakers say *"continue until the end"*. | *"It's not enough to generate ideas; we need leaders who follow through."* |
| **Usher in** | Signal or cause the start of a transformative new era. | Spanish: *"Dar paso a / inaugurar"*. | *"The new platform will usher in a period of unprecedented collaboration."* |

#### Exercise 8.4: Rapid Executive Synthesis (4 mins)
*Alicia combines expressions in spontaneous answers to Lewis:*
1. "How will your strategic initiative transform the company?"
   ➔ Must use **usher in** + **at the forefront** + **by and large**.
2. "How will you ensure that decisions made today don't create future risks?"
   ➔ Must use **set a precedent** + **follow through** + **bear testimony to**.

### Phase 4: Capstone Simulation Lab (28:00 – 45:00)
**Theme**: *The Executive Keynote & Live Press Conference / Boardroom Defense.*

#### Scenario Overview
- **Alicia's Role**: Senior Executive presenting a 3-minute impromptu Keynote to an all-hands summit announcing a major organizational pivot, followed by a live, unscripted Q&A press conference.
- **Lewis's Role**: The Press Corps / Skeptical Board Members. Fires unpredictable, challenging questions designed to provoke translation lag, stress, and hesitation.

#### Step 1: The 3-Minute Keynote Address (3 mins)
- Alicia speaks continuously for 3 minutes without written notes.
- Must demonstrate:
  - The 4 Ps (Power, dramatic Pauses, varied Pace, expressive Pitch).
  - Effortless connected speech (linking /r/, intrusive /r/, schwa, elision).
  - Target leadership vocabulary (*usher in, at the forefront, by and large, follow through*).

#### Step 2: The Live Q&A Barrage (12 mins)
- Lewis launches an aggressive, fast-paced Q&A:
  - *Q1*: *"Alicia, critics say this pivot is far too hasty and you're cutting corners. How do you respond?"*
  - *Q2*: *"Won't this set a precedent that terrifies our existing conservative clients?"*
  - *Q3*: *"What will you say to department heads who are currently at loggerheads over these changes?"*
  - *Q4*: *"If this strategy doesn't pan out, who will take the blame?"*
- **Constraint**: Alicia must respond within 2 seconds to every question, using British diplomatic framing (*"To be fair...", "Quite frankly...", "In a nutshell..."*) and zero Spanish translation hesitation.
