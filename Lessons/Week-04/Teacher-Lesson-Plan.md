# Week 4: Teacher Lesson Plan
## Vowel-to-Vowel Linking & Eliminating Glottal Stops (/j/ and /w/ Glides)

- **Student**: Alicia M. (C1 Advanced)
- **Tutor**: Lewis O.
- **Duration**: 50 Minutes
- **Target Accent Model**: Modern Received Pronunciation (RP) / Standard Southern British English (SSBE)
- **Core Text Reference**: *Get Rid of your Accent (Part Two)*, Lesson 12: Liaison of Vowel to Vowel (pp. 47–49) & Lesson 4: Glottal Stops (p. 23).

---

## Lesson Objectives
By the end of this 50-minute session, Alicia will:
1. **Eliminate Glottal Stops (/ʔ/) between Vowels**: Replace acoustic breaks with smooth, continuous vocalization.
2. **Execute Intrusive /j/ (Yod Glide)**: Seamlessly link front vowels (/iː, ɪ, eɪ, aɪ, ɔɪ/) to subsequent vowels (*see‿[j]‿it*, *the‿[j]‿apple*, *say‿[j]‿it*).
3. **Execute Intrusive /w/ Glide**: Seamlessly link back/rounded vowels (/uː, ʊ, əʊ, aʊ/) to subsequent vowels (*go‿[w]‿out*, *do‿[w]‿it*, *you‿[w]‿are*).
4. **Master the Spanish /b/ vs. /v/ and /s/ vs. /z/ Phonetic Distinctions**: Ensure labiodental friction for /v/ and proper voicing for terminal /z/.
5. **Deploy British Agility Collocations & Phrasal Verbs**: Use *spot on*, *chuffed to bits*, *in a nutshell*, *off the cuff*, *brush up on*, *step up*, and *wind down*.
6. **Deliver an Impromptu "Off the Cuff" Monologue**: Speak spontaneously about career transitions and imposter syndrome with zero hesitations.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Instructions & Exercise Banks

### Phase 1: Anti-Translation Activation & Speed Warm-Up (00:00 – 08:00)
**Tutor Goal**: Train Alicia to speak spontaneously "off the cuff" without mental outlining or Spanish translation lag.

#### Exercise 4.1: The "Off the Cuff" Rapid Opinion Sprint (4 mins)
*Lewis gives Alicia an unexpected prompt; she has 2 seconds to launch into an impromptu 30-second speech without stopping.*
1. **Prompt 1**: *"Should companies force employees back into the office full-time, or is remote work here to stay?"*
   ➔ *Constraint*: Must begin with: *"Well, speaking off the cuff, in a nutshell..."*
2. **Prompt 2**: *"What is the most overrated trait in modern corporate leadership?"*
   ➔ *Constraint*: Must begin with: *"To be completely fair, quite frankly..."*

#### Exercise 4.2: The /b/ vs. /v/ Articulatory Calibration (4 mins)
*Spanish native transfer causes speakers to use a soft bilabial sound [β] for both 'b' and 'v'.*
- **Articulatory Mechanics**:
  - `/b/` = **Both lips pressed together firmly**, holding air, then bursting open: `b-b-b` (*best, ban, berry*).
  - `/v/` = **Top front teeth resting gently on the bottom lip**, blowing voiced air continuously: `vvv-vvv-vvv` (*vest, van, very*).
- *Rapid-Fire Minimal Pair Repetition*:
  - *berry* vs. *very*
  - *ban* vs. *van*
  - *best* vs. *vest*
  - *curb* vs. *curve*
  - *boat* vs. *vote*
- *Alicia reads aloud*: *"I am **very** confident that the **best** strategy is to **vote** on the **curve**."*

### Phase 2: Pronunciation & Connected Speech Clinic (08:00 – 22:00)
**Target**: Vowel-to-Vowel Linking with Intrusive /j/ and /w/ (James & Smith, Lesson 12, pp. 47–49).

#### Theoretical Concept (2 mins)
Explain the acoustic mechanics:
- When native English speakers encounter two vowels meeting across word boundaries, they never stop the voice with a glottal catch (*see... it* ➔ /siː ʔ ɪt/).
- Instead, the mouth naturally creates an acoustic bridge (a glide):
  1. If the first word ends in a **high front vowel** (lips spread: /iː, ɪ, eɪ, aɪ, ɔɪ/), the bridge is a tiny **[j]** (like "yes").
  2. If the first word ends in a **rounded back vowel** (lips rounded: /uː, ʊ, əʊ, aʊ/), the bridge is a tiny **[w]** (like "we").

#### Exercise 4.3: Intrusive /j/ (Yod Glide) Clinic (5 mins)
*(Adapted from James & Smith, p. 47)*
*Read aloud. Emphasize the subtle [j] sound joining the words:*

**Word Pairs with [j]:**
- see ‿ [j] ‿ it ➔ `/siː-jɪt/`
- the ‿ [j] ‿ apple ➔ `/ðiː-jæp-əl/`
- say ‿ [j] ‿ it ➔ `/seɪ-jɪt/`
- my ‿ [j] ‿ own ➔ `/maɪ-jəʊn/`
- three ‿ [j] ‿ eggs ➔ `/θriː-jeɡz/`
- buy ‿ [j] ‿ it ➔ `/baɪ-jɪt/`
- high ‿ [j] ‿ energy ➔ `/haɪ-jenədʒi/`

**Sentences with [j]:**
1. *I can see ‿ [j] ‿ it clearly in my ‿ [j] ‿ own mind.*
2. *Please say ‿ [j] ‿ it ‿ again so I can write ‿ it down.*
3. *She had three ‿ [j] ‿ interviews and bought ‿ a brand new car.*

#### Exercise 4.4: Intrusive /w/ (Labial Glide) Clinic (5 mins)
*(Adapted from James & Smith, p. 48)*
*Read aloud. Feel the lips round into a subtle [w] sound:*

**Word Pairs with [w]:**
- go ‿ [w] ‿ out ➔ `/ɡəʊ-waʊt/`
- do ‿ [w] ‿ it ➔ `/duː-wɪt/`
- you ‿ [w] ‿ are ➔ `/juː-wɑː/`
- two ‿ [w] ‿ others ➔ `/tuː-wʌðəz/`
- now ‿ [w] ‿ and then ➔ `/naʊ-wən-ðen/`
- so ‿ [w] ‿ often ➔ `/səʊ-wɒf-tən/`
- throw ‿ [w] ‿ it ➔ `/θrəʊ-wɪt/`

**Sentences with [w]:**
1. *Let's go ‿ [w] ‿ out and do ‿ [w] ‿ it right away.*
2. *You ‿ [w] ‿ are one ‿ of the two ‿ [w] ‿ others chosen.*
3. *Now ‿ [w] ‿ and then, throw ‿ [w] ‿ it ‿ away.*

#### Exercise 4.5: Final /s/ vs. /z/ Voicing Drill (2 mins)
*Spanish speakers tend to devoice all final sibilants to /s/.*
*Ensure vocal cord vibration for /z/:*
- *advice* (/s/) vs. *advise* (/z/)
- *price* (/s/) vs. *prize* (/z/)
- *ice* (/s/) vs. *eyes* (/z/)
- *buses* (/s-ɪz/) vs. *buzzes* (/z-ɪz/)
*Read aloud*: *"His eyes (/z/) advised (/z/) the team on business strategies."*

### Phase 3: British Lexical Upgrade (22:00 – 32:00)
**Theme**: Colloquial Wit, Precision & Professional Transitions.

#### Core Lexicon Matrix

| Expression | Meaning & Nuance | Spanish Translation Pitfall | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spot on** | Completely accurate; precise; exactly right. | Spanish speakers say *"exactly"* or *"it is correct"*. | *"Your analysis of the market trends was absolutely spot on."* |
| **Chuffed to bits** | Thoroughly pleased, proud, or delighted. | Spanish speakers say *"very happy"* or *"very satisfied"*. | *"She was chuffed to bits when she landed the new role."* |
| **In a nutshell** | Concluded or summarized in a few precise words. | Spanish speakers say *"in resume"* (false friend from *resumen*). | *"In a nutshell, we exceeded our targets despite the downturn."* |
| **Off the cuff** | Spoken spontaneously without preparation or notes. | Spanish speakers say *"improvised"* or *"spontaneously"*. | *"I had to give a presentation completely off the cuff."* |
| **Brush up on** | Refresh or polish an existing skill or body of knowledge. | Spanish speakers say *"review"* or *"re-learn"*. | *"I need to brush up on my data analytics before next week."* |
| **Step up** | Take on greater responsibility or meet a challenging standard. | Spanish speakers say *"assume responsibility"* or *"advance"*. | *"When the director resigned, Alicia stepped up to lead the team."* |
| **Wind down** | Gradually relax after work; or slowly conclude an operation. | Spanish speakers say *"relax"* or *"finish slowly"*. | *"After a hectic week, I like to wind down with a good book."* |

#### Exercise 4.6: In-Context Collocation Construction (4 mins)
*Alicia combines target expressions in spontaneous answers to Lewis:*
1. "How do you prepare when asked to speak unexpectedly in a meeting?"
   ➔ Must use **off the cuff** + **in a nutshell**.
2. "How are you preparing for your upcoming job?"
   ➔ Must use **brush up on** + **step up** + **chuffed to bits**.

### Phase 4: High-Level Discussion Lab (32:00 – 47:00)
**Theme**: *Imposter Syndrome, Career Transitions & The Unwritten Rules of Corporate Culture.*

#### Scenario & Discussion Questions
- **Context**: Alicia is preparing to enter her new company. Starting a senior position often brings psychological challenges: adapting to new office politics, proving competence, and overcoming imposter syndrome.
- **Discussion Prompts**:
  1. *Psychology of Imposter Syndrome*: Why do high-achieving professionals often feel like frauds when stepping into a larger role? Is it humility or self-sabotage?
  2. *The Unwritten Corporate Playbook*: In British and international workplaces, how much of success depends on technical skill vs. relational intelligence and diplomatic communication?
  3. *Speaking Off the Cuff*: Why does speaking spontaneously in a second language provoke anxiety, and what mental shift is required to let go of perfectionism?

#### Teacher Facilitation & Flow Management
- Challenge Alicia to argue both sides:
  - *"Some people say imposter syndrome is healthy because it prevents arrogance. Do you agree?"*
- Track her vocal glides:
  - Did she connect vowels seamlessly (*see‿[j]‿it*, *do‿[w]‿it*, *go‿[w]‿out*)?
  - Did she maintain distinct /b/ vs. /v/ articulation when discussing *very*, *valuable*, *business*, *best*?
