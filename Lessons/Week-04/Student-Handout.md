## 1. Core Pronunciation Mechanics

### A. Eliminating Glottal Stops Between Vowels
In English, adjacent vowels are never separated by a sudden catch or halt in the throat (a glottal stop). Instead, native British speakers insert a subtle **glide** to bridge the vowels:

1. **Intrusive [j] (Yod Glide)**:
   - When the first word ends in a spread front vowel: `/iː/`, `/ɪ/`, `/eɪ/`, `/aɪ/`, `/ɔɪ/`.
   - Practice:
     - `see ‿ [j] ‿ it` ➔ /siː-jɪt/
     - `the ‿ [j] ‿ apple` ➔ /ðiː-jæp-əl/
     - `my ‿ [j] ‿ own` ➔ /maɪ-jəʊn/
     - `say ‿ [j] ‿ it` ➔ /seɪ-jɪt/
     - `three ‿ [j] ‿ eggs` ➔ /θriː-jeɡz/

2. **Intrusive [w] (Labial Glide)**:
   - When the first word ends in a rounded back vowel: `/uː/`, `/ʊ/`, `/əʊ/`, `/aʊ/`.
   - Practice:
     - `go ‿ [w] ‿ out` ➔ /ɡəʊ-waʊt/
     - `do ‿ [w] ‿ it` ➔ /duː-wɪt/
     - `you ‿ [w] ‿ are` ➔ /juː-wɑː/
     - `now ‿ [w] ‿ and then` ➔ /naʊ-wən-ðen/
     - `two ‿ [w] ‿ hours` ➔ /tuː-waʊəz/

### B. Spanish Interference Clinic: /b/ vs. /v/
- **/b/**: Bilabial plosive. Both lips press firmly together, blocking the air, then release with a burst: `b-b-b` (*best, boat, berry*).
- **/v/**: Labiodental fricative. Upper front teeth rest gently on the lower lip while you vocalize continuously: `vvv-vvv-vvv` (*vest, vote, very*).

## 2. British Lexical Upgrade: Agility, Wit & Transitions

| Expression | Meaning | Natural British Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spot on** | Exactly accurate; completely right. | *"Her assessment of the project risks was spot on."* |
| **Chuffed to bits** | Delighted; deeply pleased or proud. | *"I was chuffed to bits to receive the formal offer."* |
| **In a nutshell** | Summarized concisely in very few words. | *"In a nutshell, we need to increase client retention."* |
| **Off the cuff** | Spontaneous; spoken without prior notes or preparation. | *"He delivered a fantastic presentation entirely off the cuff."* |
| **Brush up on** | To quickly refresh or polish an existing skill. | *"I'm going to brush up on my data visualization skills."* |
| **Step up** | To rise to a challenge and accept greater responsibility. | *"The team needed leadership, so she stepped up."* |
| **Wind down** | To relax gradually after intense effort; or conclude operations. | *"It takes me an hour to wind down after an intense day."* |
