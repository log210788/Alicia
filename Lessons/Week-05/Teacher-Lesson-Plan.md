## Lesson Objectives
By the end of this 50-minute session, Alicia will:
1. **Master Non-Rhotic Vowel Elongation**: Suppress the urge to pronounce post-vocalic /r/ before consonants or at sentence ends (*car* /kɑː/, *work* /wɜːk/, *water* /ˈwɔːtə/).
2. **Execute Linking /r/**: Seamlessly bridge terminal orthographic "r" into following vowels (*car‿r‿insurance*, *far‿r‿away*, *better‿r‿off*).
3. **Master Intrusive /r/**: Produce natural British intrusive /r/ between vowels where no letter "r" exists (*law‿r‿and order*, *the idea‿r‿of it*, *media‿r‿attention*).
4. **Deploy Political & Media Discourse Expressions**: Use *entrenched views*, *at loggerheads*, *stir up*, *play down*, *call out*, and *zero in on*.
5. **Debate the Trial Lesson Theme**: Engage in a high-level philosophical debate on societal polarization, media orchestration, and civil discourse.

### Phase 2: Pronunciation & Connected Speech Clinic (08:00 – 22:00)
**Target**: Non-Rhoticity, Linking /r/, and Intrusive /r/ (James & Smith, Lessons 13 & 14).

#### Theoretical Concept (2 mins)
Explain the British R-System:
1. **Non-Rhotic Rule**: In British RP, the letter "r" is **silent** unless followed immediately by a vowel sound. Vowels before "r" become longer and richer (*card* ➔ /kɑːd/, *bird* ➔ /bɜːd/).
2. **Linking /r/**: If a word ends in the letter "r" and the *next* word begins with a vowel, the /r/ is "awakened" and pronounced to link the words (*car‿r‿accident* ➔ /ˈkɑːr-æk.sɪ.dənt/).
3. **Intrusive /r/**: When a word ends with /ə/, /ɔː/, or /ɑː/ and has **NO** letter "r", native British speakers insert a phantom /r/ sound before a following vowel to avoid a clumsy glottal stop (*law‿r‿and order* ➔ /ˈlɔːr-ənd-ɔːdə/).

#### Exercise 5.3: Linking /r/ Drills (5 mins)
*(Adapted from James & Smith, Lesson 13, p. 50)*
*Read aloud. Connect the terminal "r" directly onto the next vowel sound:*

**Word Pairs with Linking /r/:**
- car ‿ r ‿ insurance ➔ `/kɑː-rɪn-ʃʊərəns/`
- car ‿ r ‿ accident ➔ `/kɑː-ræk-sɪdənt/`
- under ‿ r ‿ age ➔ `/ʌn-də-reɪdʒ/`
- under ‿ r ‿ arrest ➔ `/ʌn-də-rə-rest/`
- far ‿ r ‿ away ➔ `/fɑː-rə-weɪ/`
- better ‿ r ‿ off ➔ `/be-tə-rɒf/`
- for ‿ r ‿ ever and ever ➔ `/fə-re-və-rən-devə/`
- fair ‿ r ‿ enough ➔ `/feə-rɪ-nʌf/`

**Sentences with Linking /r/:**
1. *Where ‿ r ‿ are you working today?*
2. *Her car ‿ r ‿ is in the garage all day.*
3. *We were better ‿ r ‿ off last year.*
4. *Thank you so much for ‿ r ‿ everything.*
5. *We are ‿ r ‿ away in Winchester ‿ r ‿ all next week.*

#### Exercise 5.4: Intrusive /r/ Drills (5 mins)
*(Adapted from James & Smith, Lesson 14, p. 53)*
*Notice: There is NO letter "r" in the spelling! Insert a subtle, fluid [r] between the vowels:*

**Word Pairs with Intrusive /r/:**
- law ‿ [r] ‿ and order ➔ `/lɔː-r-ənd-ɔːdə/`
- the idea ‿ [r] ‿ of it ➔ `/ðiː aɪ-dɪə-r-əv ɪt/`
- media ‿ [r] ‿ attention ➔ `/miː-di-ə-r-ə-ten-ʃən/`
- draw ‿ [r] ‿ a line ➔ `/drɔː-r-ə-laɪn/`
- China ‿ [r] ‿ and Japan ➔ `/tʃaɪ-nə-r-ənd dʒə-pæn/`
- raw ‿ [r] ‿ emotion ➔ `/rɔː-r-ɪ-məʊ-ʃən/`
- drama ‿ [r] ‿ and theatre ➔ `/drɑː-mə-r-ənd θɪə-tə/`

**Sentences with Intrusive /r/:**
1. *The law ‿ [r] ‿ and order situation caused raw ‿ [r] ‿ emotion.*
2. *I have no idea ‿ [r] ‿ of what the media ‿ [r] ‿ is planning.*
3. *Let's draw ‿ [r] ‿ a line under the past.*

### Phase 3: British Lexical Upgrade (22:00 – 32:00)
**Theme**: Political Polarization, Media & Societal Discourse (Trial Lesson Vocabulary Expansion).

#### Core Lexicon Matrix

| Expression | Meaning & Nuance | Spanish Translation Pitfall | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Entrenched views** | Deeply rooted opinions that refuse to compromise. | Spanish speakers say *"fixed opinions"* or *"closed minds"*. | *"Both political parties have entrenched views on immigration."* |
| **At loggerheads** | In persistent, stubborn disagreement or conflict. | Spanish speakers say *"fighting"* or *"in confrontation"*. | *"The union and management are at loggerheads over wages."* |
| **Stir up** | Deliberately provoke controversy, unrest, or trouble. | Spanish speakers say *"provoke"* or *"create problems"*. | *"Sensationalist headlines are designed to stir up outrage."* |
| **Play down** | Minimize or diminish the apparent seriousness of an issue. | Spanish speakers say *"minimize"* or *"underestimate"*. | *"The government tried to play down the economic crisis."* |
| **Call out** | Publicly challenge or expose someone's dishonest or unethical behavior. | Spanish speakers say *"criticize publicly"* or *"denounce"*. | *"Journalists should call out politicians who spread disinformation."* |
| **Zero in on** | Focus one's full analytical or conversational attention onto a specific point. | Spanish speakers say *"focus in"* (error: *in* instead of *on*). | *"Let's zero in on the underlying cause of public frustration."* |

#### Exercise 5.5: Lexical Precision Drill (4 mins)
*Have Alicia replace the basic italicized word with the target British phrase:*
1. "The minister tried to *minimize* the protest." ➔ **played down**.
2. "Social media algorithms *provoke* angry division." ➔ **stir up**.
3. "The two ministers are *strongly fighting* over policy." ➔ **at loggerheads**.
4. "The report *focused precisely on* government complacency." ➔ **zeroed in on**.

### Phase 4: High-Level Debate Lab (32:00 – 47:00)
**Theme**: *Echo Chambers, Political Tensions & The Role of Modern Media.*
*(Directly expanding upon her trial lesson: "tensions are high between the left and right", "complacent government", "orchestrated narratives").*

#### Scenario & Debate Structure
- **Debate Motion**: *"Online media does not inform citizens; it deliberately stirs up outrage and entrenches societal division for advertising revenue."*
- **Alicia's Position**: Defends the motion. Argues that public complacency has been replaced by weaponized algorithmic outrage; people with entrenched views are at loggerheads because nuance has been eliminated.
- **Lewis's Position**: Opposes the motion. Argues that social media simply democratizes speech; citizens were always divided, but now ordinary people can call out powerful institutions directly.

#### Speech Constraints & Integration Goals
- Alicia must naturally integrate:
  - Linking /r/: *where‿r‿are they*, *better‿r‿off*, *for‿r‿ever*.
  - Intrusive /r/: *media‿[r]‿attention*, *the idea‿[r]‿of it*, *law‿[r]‿and order*.
  - Target vocabulary: *entrenched views, stir up, play down, at loggerheads, zero in on*.

#### Debate Prompts & Cross-Examination
- Lewis: *"Isn't it true that citizens can now call out government complacency faster than ever before?"*
- Alicia rebuts: *"To be fair, while citizens can call them out, algorithms immediately zero in on extreme voices to stir up emotion, which leaves society at loggerheads..."*
